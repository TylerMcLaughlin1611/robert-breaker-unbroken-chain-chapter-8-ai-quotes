How to read this

Each entry gives the quotation exactly as it appears in the book, the citation the book gives for it, and what the cited source actually contains. Where the real text is available, it is reproduced so readers can compare for themselves.

A quotation in quotation marks is a claim that those are the words in the source. That is the standard each entry is held to.

Fake

The words are not in the cited source, the cited source does not exist, or the historical record says something different.

Only source is Robert Breaker

The quote appears nowhere except Breaker’s book. The source it is credited to (an archive, a broadcast, an unpublished sermon) is not public, so it cannot be checked directly.

Church Fathers & early medieval

354 to 800
01

Augustine of Hippo

354 to 430 A.D.Fake · Misattributed
As quoted in the book All my hope, and the certainty of my faith, is in the precious blood of Christ, which was shed for us and for our salvation.
Finding

This is an incorrectly attributed quote. The misinformation stems from Alphonsus Liguori, a Catholic Saint and Italian Bishop of the Roman Catholic Church, who said in his Prayer: The Great Means of Salvation and of Perfection:

What Liguori actually wrote
“But St. Augustine had no doubt when he said, ‘All my hope, and the certainty of my faith, is in the Precious Blood of Christ, Which was shed for us and for our salvation.’ Thus the Saint placed all his hope in the Blood of Jesus Christ; because the faith assured him that Christ died for all.”
Alphonsus Liguori, Prayer: The Great Means of Salvation and of Perfection
02

Gennadius of Massilia

c. 495 A.D.Fake
As quoted in the book Christ died once for all. The faith which saves is faith in His blood. This cannot be earned, nor bought, nor repeated in the Mass, but only believed and received. Cited as De Ecclesiasticis Dogmatibus, Chapter 45.
Finding

Chapter 45 of De Ecclesiasticis Dogmatibus is about free will and grace. It does not say what Breaker says it does.

What Chapter 45 actually says
If anyone contends that some can come to the grace of baptism by mercy, and others by free will, which, in all who are born of the first man’s transgression, is shown to be vitiated, he is proved to be alien to the right faith. For he does not assert that the free will of all was weakened by the sin of the first man; or at least he thinks it so impaired that some can nevertheless, without the revelation of God, by their own efforts seek out the ministry of eternal salvation. How contrary this is, the Lord himself proves (John 6:44), who testifies that not some, but no one can come to him, unless the Father has drawn him. As he also says to Peter: “Blessed are you, Simon son of Jonah, for this was not revealed to you by flesh and blood, but by my Father in heaven” (Matthew 16:17). And the Apostle: No one can say, “Lord Jesus,” except in the Holy Spirit (1 Cor. 12:3).
Gennadius, De Ecclesiasticis Dogmatibus 45 (English translation)
Original Latin

Si quis alios per misericordiam, alios vero per liberum arbitrium, quod in omnibus qui de praevaricatione primi hominis nati sunt constat esse vitiatum, ad gratiam baptismi posse venire contendit, a recta fide probatur alienus. Ille enim non omnium liberum arbitrium per peccatum primi hominis asserit infirmatum; aut certe ita laesum putat, ut tamen quidam valeant sine revelatione Dei ministerium salutis aeternae per semetipsos posse conquirere. Quod quam sit contrarium ipse Dominus probat (Joan. VI, 44), qui non aliquos, sed neminem ad se posse venire testatur, nisi quem Pater attraxerit. Sicut et Petro dicit: Beatus es, Simon Bar Jona, quia caro et sanguis non revelavit tibi, sed Pater meus, qui in coelis est (Matth. XVI, 17). Et Apostolus: Nemo potest dicere Dominus Jesus, nisi in Spiritu sancto (I Cor. XII, 3).

03

Cassiodorus

Italy, c. 510 A.D.Fake
As quoted in the book We are reconciled to God by the blood of His Son, not by merit. Let the sinner trust in that blood, and not despair. It is not fasting or relics, but faith in the blood which saves. Cited as Explanation of the Psalms, Vol. 1, Psalm 51.
Finding

Cassiodorus: Explanation of the Psalms Vol. 1 only contains Psalms 1 to 50 (which is actually 51). Volume 2 contains 51 (which actually starts with 52) to 100, and Volume 3 contains 101 to 150. The Vulgate and Septuagint use a numbering system which is offset one number from the Hebrew Masoretic ordering.

Giving this the benefit of the doubt, let’s assume the quote only rejects the Catholic ordering. His commentary in Volume 1 on the book’s Psalm 50 (which is actually 51) contains no such passage.

And assume for the sake of argument it is actually the Catholic ordering, and it’s the Hebrew Masoretic Psalm 52:

  1. You can more or less see every page describing his commentary on Psalm 52, and it says nothing of the kind.
  2. Why would he mention the blood in a verse about God’s judgment on Doeg the Edomite?

Cassiodorus is used as one of the first people to mention the Catholic relic of the crown of thorns, making it highly illogical he would be against relics.

06

Bede the Venerable

England, c. 730 A.D.Fake
As quoted in the book The blood of Christ cries out for mercy… And those who believe in that blood shall find peace with God. The cross is the altar, and the faithful are justified through trust in the sacrifice made there. Cited as Commentary on 1 Pet. 1:2.
Finding

Bede’s actual commentary on 1 Peter 1:2 says nothing like this. It speaks of election, sanctification by the Spirit, and sprinkling with the blood after the pattern of the Passover lamb.

What Bede actually wrote
According to the foreknowledge of God the Father, etc. These verses are joined to what he had said before: “to the elect strangers.” For they were elect according to the foreknowledge of God the Father. Hence the apostle says: ‘Whom he foreknew he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son.’ And elsewhere: As he chose us in him before the foundation of the world. The elect, however, were chosen for this purpose, that through the gift of the Holy Spirit they should be sanctified, cleansed from all sins, so that they, who had perished through the disobedience of the first-formed man, might begin to obey the Lord Jesus Christ, and that, sprinkled with his blood, they might avoid the power of Satan, just as Israel, by the blood of the lamb, cast off the dominion of the Egyptians. He speaks of sprinkling, however, in the manner of the Old Testament, where those things that were to be sanctified were accustomed to be sprinkled with the blood of sacrifices. Moreover, the Lord himself speaks plainly of it: ‘Unless you have eaten…’
Bede, Super Epistolas Catholicas, on 1 Peter 1:2 (translated with DeepL, lightly corrected)
Original Latin

Secundum praescientiam Dei Patris, etc. Adhaerent hi versiculi ad hoc quod praedixerat, Electis advenis. Electi namque erant secundum praescientiam Dei Patris. Unde et apostolus ait: Quos praescivit et praedestinavit conformes fieri imaginis Filii sui. Et alibi: Sicut elegit nos in ipso ante mundi constitutionem. Electi autem erant ad hoc, ut per donationem Spiritus sancti sanctificarentur, emundati ab omnibus peccatis, ut obedire inciperent Domino Jesu Christo, qui per inobedientiam protoplasti perierant, ut aspersi sanguine ejus potestatem Satanae vitarent, sicut Israel per agni sanguinem Aegyptorum dominatum declinavit. Aspersionem autem dicit more Scripturae veteris, ubi quae sanctificanda erant, sanguine hostiarum solebant aspergi. Caeterum ipse Dominus dicit manifeste de eo: Nisi manducaveritis…

08

The Paulicians

Eastern Europe, 7th to 11th centuriesFake
As quoted in the book We are justified not by works, nor by prayers to saints, nor by images, but by the blood of Christ shed once for all. And by faith we receive that blood, and are made whole. Cited as Quoted in Armenian chroniclers, Key of Truth, translated by F.C. Conybeare, 1898.
Finding

Conybeare’s 1898 English translation of The Key of Truth was fully searched through. The sentence is not in it, and neither are its distinctive phrases: “justified not by works,” “prayers to saints,” “shed once for all,” “receive that blood” and “made whole” do not appear.

  1. The citation is wrong. The Key of Truth is not a chronicle. It is an anonymous Paulician manual, which Conybeare published as A Manual of the Paulician Church of Armenia. The “Armenian chroniclers” are the medieval churchmen Conybeare quotes in his introduction and appendices, and most of them were hostile to the Paulicians.
  2. The real text teaches something different. It names four things that save, and baptism and the body and blood of the Lord’s Supper are two of them.
What The Key of Truth actually says
“Four are they which save man. First, Repentance. Second, Right Faith. Third, Holy Baptism.… And fourth, the holy precious body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ.”
The Key of Truth, trans. F.C. Conybeare (Oxford, 1898), Catechism

Fulbert of Chartres

France, c. 1025 A.D.Fake
As quoted in the book Jesus Christ is the Lamb, and His blood is the cleansing for our sins. We approach not by ritual alone, but by faith in His wounds, for in them is peace and reconciliation. Cited as Sermon in Passione Domini.
Finding

He has 9 surviving sermons, and this is not one of them. He also taught the real presence.

What Fulbert of Chartres actually taught
“But Fulbert adhered to the traditional orthodoxy, and expressed himself against innovations, in letters to his metropolitan, Leutberich, archbishop of Sens. He regarded the real presence as an object of faith and adoration rather than of curious speculation, but thought that it is not more difficult to believe in a transformation of substance by Divine power than in the creation of substance. He was a zealous worshipper of the saints, especially of the Virgin Mary, and one of the first who celebrated the festival of her Nativity.”
Philip Schaff, History of the Christian Church, Vol. 4

The Bogomils

Balkans, 10th to 13th centuriesFake
As quoted in the book We reject the Mass and the cross as idols, but we believe in the death of Jesus Christ and in His blood shed for sinners. The true altar is the heart that believes in the Lamb. Cited as The Secret Book of the Bogomils, quoted in Emmanuel d’Hooghvorst’s analysis, 1991.
Finding

“Baron Emmanuel van der Linden d’Hooghvorst (1914–1999) was a Belgian writer, spagyric philosopher and alchemist.”

  1. d’Hooghvorst did not publish any books in 1991, and he believed in Kabbalism. Why would he write on the Bogomils?
  2. The citation is illogical on further analysis: why would you quote him instead of just quoting the primary source?
  3. The Secret Book of the Bogomils does not have this line.
NoteThe person who fact checked Chapter 8 (I have since de-associated from him) was alarmed by the Bogomil entry, and he thought Milena Varzonovtseva wrote The Secret Book of the Bogomils. She did not. Her book is about something else entirely, and it would not be applicable to what Robert Breaker is saying: “This book explores the widely assumed, but rarely investigated connection between two medieval documents: the eschatological pseudepigraphon 2 Enoch and the main text of the Bogomils, their Secret Book.”

Antonio Herrezuelo

Martyr of Valladolid, 1559Fake
As quoted in the book I have committed no crime, but have trusted in Christ alone for salvation. Let the flames bear witness that I reject the Mass and all its idols, and that I trust in the blood of Christ alone. Cited as Eyewitness account of his final words as he was burned alive. Finding
  1. The book does not exist. Leonard Verduin never wrote a book called Spanish Protestant Martyrs.
  2. Herrezuelo is recorded as having been gagged the whole time he burned. A man who was gagged could not have given these final words.
23

Leonor de Cisneros

Burned 1568Fake
As quoted in the book I denied the Lord once, but I will not deny Him again. Christ died for me, and I am saved by His blood, not by the Mass, not by confession to a priest. Let them kill me. Cited as Statement upon voluntarily returning to the Inquisition for martyrdom.
Finding

The book gives no source, and no account of her life records any statement by her, at her trial or at the stake. The only place these words appear is Breaker’s book.

Leonor, Herrezuelo’s wife, recanted in 1559 and was sentenced to three years’ imprisonment in a Benedictine monastery. After her husband’s death she returned to her Protestant faith and openly spoke about it among the other prisoners. She was condemned as a relapsed heretic and burned at Valladolid on September 26, 1568. So she did not walk back into the Inquisition from outside, as the book’s framing suggests.

Her story is well documented, and nowhere in it does she say anything like this. A 16th-century martyr’s words that first appear in a book published in 2025, with no source, have no historical basis.

Revivalists & modern preachers

1734 to 1977
24

Jonathan Edwards

1703 to 1758, New EnglandFake
As quoted in the book It is by faith in the blood of Christ that sinners are justified. The blood is the price, the ransom, the satisfaction. And faith is the hand that receives the atonement. The blood does not save automatically; it must be applied. And that is the office of faith: to lay hold of the blood and plead it before the throne. Cited as Justification by Faith Alone, sermon preached in 1734.
Finding

The full text of Justification by Faith Alone (dated November 1734) was checked word for word. None of these sentences appear in it. The phrases “lay hold of the blood,” “plead it before the throne,” and “does not save automatically” are absent, and the word “ransom” does not appear anywhere in the discourse.

The nearest real line is about faith in general, not about blood: faith is “the instrument, or as it were the hand, by which he that has purchased justification is apprehended and accepted.”

What Edwards actually taught about the blood
“Therefore the Scripture seems everywhere to attribute the whole of salvation to the blood of Christ. This precious blood is as much the main price by which heaven is purchased, as it is the main price by which we are redeemed from hell.”
Jonathan Edwards, Justification by Faith Alone (1734/1738)

Billy Sunday

1862 to 1935, evangelistFake
As quoted in the book When Jesus Christ shed His blood on the cross, that was the payment for sin. But it won’t do you any good until you believe in it. E.J. Pace

1879 to 1946Fake
As quoted in the book What can wash away my sin? Nothing but the blood of Jesus. And how is that blood applied? Through faith! The gospel is not that Jesus lived, but that He died and shed His blood, and we must trust that blood to be cleansed. Romans 3:25 settles the issue. Without faith in the blood, you are still in your sins. Cited as From an unpublished sermon illustration in The Fundamentals, Vol. 3, Testimony Publishing, 1910. The book describes him as a “Baptist Illustrator, Bible Teacher and Theologian.”
Finding
  1. The citation contradicts itself. A sermon illustration cannot be both “unpublished” and printed in The Fundamentals.
  2. Pace did not write for The Fundamentals. Volume 3 (1910) contains essays by Gray, Moorehead, Speer, Mullins, Whitelaw, Reeve and Studd.
  3. In 1910 Pace was a missionary in the Philippines (1905 to 1915). His cartoons first appeared in The Sunday School Times on December 2, 1916.
  4. He was not a Baptist. He was an ordained United Brethren minister.
  5. The opening lines are Robert Lowry’s hymn “Nothing But the Blood.”

Ian Paisley

1926 to 2014Only source is Robert Breaker

Almost every 20th-century quotation in this chapter cites Romans 3:25 by name. Bob Jones Sr., R.G. Lee, Carl McIntire, W.A. Criswell, Ian Paisley and the E.J. Pace entry all do. These men came from different denominations and different decades, yet each one leans on the exact verse Breaker builds his doctrine on.

That is what a chatbot produces when it is asked for quotes supporting “faith in His blood.”
